This project came out of Engineering for Humanity, an Olin class in which engineering students form a design team with an older adult in the local community to solve a problem of daily life. Linda used an oxygen tank, and our team focused on that. 

{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h1.png" %}

She had been carrying her oxygen in the backpack shown above, which was issued with the tank. Through our collaborative design process, we created a custom solution.

{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h2.png" %}

This is one of my sketches illustrating Linda's idea to have the top of the bag flip open and closed, rather than closing with a zipper. The original bag did include a plastic grommet opening, the tubing had to be disconnected and threaded through every time, so it was easier to leave the zipper slightly open. A safer, easier to use solution for the tubing was one of our priorities for the new bag. 

{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h4.png" %}

During one of our visits to her home, I created a paper sketch model to illustrate the idea of the ‘flip top’ backpack. 

{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h3.png" %}

Borrowing an actual oxygen tank, I planned out the pattern pieces in paper. I cut these pieces in black rip-stop synthetic canvas, stiff structural interfacing, and felt for the back and bottom for additional padding. I used a sailcloth machine for assembling the back piece of the bag where all the webbing for the straps anchors to bag itself. 

{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h5.png" %}
{% include image.html image="projects/E4H/e4h6.png" %}

Linda used the bag as long as she carried oxygen. All these years later, this project remains my most meaningful expression of collaborative design.
