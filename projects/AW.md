After college, I worked as a mechanical engineer for a wind energy startup. I started as a remote intern in the summer of 2021, ended up loving the work, and continued there through the design and production of the first test prototype for model validation. 

{% include image.html image="projects/AW/AWfield1.jpg" %}

That's me doing validation testing at Windward Engineering test site in Utah. I’m about 30 feet off the ground, installing our prototype on top of a “building” made of shipping containers. 

The startup was focused on small-scale affordable wind energy for large commercial buildings. The product was an airfoil ‘spoiler’ which reshapes and increases the already faster windspeed at roof edges. A turbine is mounted above the spoiler. 

{% include image.html image="projects/AW/mockup.png" %}

We had CAD mockups and a lot of CFD, and in 2023 we were working on verification testing.

##Mechanical Design
Finally seeing the 4 meter wide aluminum assembly was almost surreal for me, because I had spent so long making it in Onshape, refining the design, running different wind loads in two different FEA programs, and sending the drawings back and forth with our manufacturers.

{% include image.html image="projects/AW/AWfield2.jpg" %}

I saw it in person for the first time when it arrived in Utah from Alabama. 
One result we needed from the half-scale prototype test was to verify that the reaction forces were in line with what we were seeing from CFD simulations.

{% include image.html image="projects/AW/AWfield3.jpg" %}

We installed a frame on the top shipping container which attached to the spoiler with load cells to measure the forces. The load cells aren’t installed in the photo, we used stand-ins so they wouldn’t be damaged. The emergency attachment post is also visible on the left.

##Anemometry
{% include image.html image="projects/AW/anem1.jpg" %}

Another of my major projects was anemometry.  
The data from the load cells needed simultaneous wind speed data to be useful. We had ultrasonic anemometers positioned upstream of the shipping containers, outside their body of influence. Another sensor array was set up at the future turbine location above the spoiler. We used ultrasonic anemometers to collect validation data, and to establish a baseline so we could asses the accuracy of other, less pricey sensors.
